Not everyone in the red is the same distance from getting out.

The calculator shows what one stated policy does to the whole county. This page asks the personal question instead: for a household already below its budget, how large a raise — on its own, nothing else changing — would put it exactly at the line?

That number falls straight out of the same formula as everywhere else on this site: (required income ÷ actual income − 1) × 100. Computed exactly for all 520,257 households below their budget, it is never a claim about any one household — every row is synthetic — but the shape of the whole distribution is exact, not estimated.

Method & limits

The formula

For each household below its budget, wage_lift_pct_needed = (hlb_year / hh_income − 1) × 100 — the smallest uniform raise that clears its own line, holding housing and childcare costs fixed. That is exactly what the calculator's wage lever alone does (cap and childcare sliders at zero), just solved for the household's own threshold instead of evaluated at one preset percentage.

Why this isn't a prediction

Nothing here is estimated or modeled from proxies. hlb_year and hh_income are both given; the raise needed is arithmetic on two known numbers, computed once for all 520,257 households and reported as a distribution. See the model we removed for why this project does not ship a classifier over the same formula.

Zero-income households

A percentage lever multiplies income by (1 + x). At an income of zero, every multiple is still zero — no raise of any size closes that household's gap. They are reported separately, never folded into the percentile distribution.

Composition categories

The same five categories used in the calculator's "who is falling short" section, so the two pages describe the same population the same way.

Cross-check against the calculator's own grid

Recomputing, at each of the calculator's nine wage steps, how many households would still be below budget reproduces data/grid.json's own shipped counts for the wage lever alone (rent cap and childcare off) — two independent code paths over the same population, the standing bar this project holds a number to before it ships.

Same caveats as everywhere else

Rows are synthetic households, cloned from 55,218 PUMS donors — valid for distributions, never for claims about individuals. No time dimension: this is a raise applied once, not wage growth over years, and implies nothing about how long that would take.
